## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Lake Worth Independent School District — traces its roots to Rosen Heights Independent School, founded in 1923 with 16 pupils in a vacant store. The district acquired land in Lake Worth in 1936 to build a school, with the building first referred to as "Lake Worth School" by May 1940; after a fire destroyed the original building, school resumed in a new (still-unfinished) building in fall 1943. Lake Worth High School is located at 4210 Boat Club Rd.
- **Recreation**: Lake Worth itself — the city's namesake reservoir, offering boating and lakeside recreation (Boat Club Road reflects this identity).
- **Civic**: Lake Worth City Hall, 3805 Adam Grubb Drive, Lake Worth, TX 76135.

## 4. History

- The roots of Lake Worth's community identity trace to 1923, when Rosen Heights Independent School opened with just 16 pupils in a donated vacant store.
- In 1936, the school district acquired land specifically within what is now Lake Worth to build a new school building, with the first documented reference to "Lake Worth School" dating to May 8, 1940.
- A fire destroyed the original school building, and in fall 1943 classes resumed in a new, still-unfinished building — reflecting the resilience of the small community during wartime.
- The City of Lake Worth incorporated in 1949 as "Lake Worth Village," formalizing the community that had grown up around the lake and its school.
- Population growth has been modest but steady since incorporation, settling into a stable range around 4,600–4,900 residents over the past quarter-century.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW metro — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Tarrant County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data); Lake Worth shares full exposure to the DFW hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Lake Worth's lakefront and near-lake neighborhoods can see elevated straight-line wind exposure off open water during spring storms, compounding shingle-lift and blow-off risk.
- **Roofing material impact**: Given the combination of regional hail intensity and lakefront wind exposure, Class 4 impact-resistant shingle upgrades are a strong fit for Lake Worth homeowners, particularly in lake-adjacent neighborhoods like Lakeview Heights.
