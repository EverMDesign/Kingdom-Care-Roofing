# Crowley, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Tarrant County (a small 5.8-acre portion extends south into Johnson County)
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 5 miles northeast of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base (driving distance; roughly 10 minutes)
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Chisholm Trail Parkway (more than one-third of the Parkway's length runs through Crowley ISD, with 18 interchanges), FM 1187, Interstate 35W (nearby access)
- **Population**: 18,070 at the 2020 Census, up 40.8% from 2010; grew from under 300 in the late 1920s to about 500 by the end of WWII, then 6,990 (1990), 7,449 (2000), 12,837 (2010), and 18,059–18,070 (2020) — one of the fastest-growing cities in the immediate Kingdom Care service area
- **Incorporation**: First settled in 1848 (city also traces founding-era roots to 1872, honoring Colonel Benjamin Franklin Crowley); incorporated as a town following a February 3, 1951 election; redesignated from town to city on September 3, 1972
- **General character**: A rapidly growing southern Tarrant County suburb directly adjacent to Burleson and Fort Worth, transformed by the Chisholm Trail Parkway from a small rural rail town into one of the area's busiest new-construction corridors.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Deer Creek Estates & Creekside**: Established family neighborhoods with a mix of 1990s–2010s housing stock, largely architectural shingle.
- **FM 1187 Corridor**: Described as the area's best new-construction value — newer homes here are predominantly builder-grade architectural shingle, with Class 4 impact-resistant options increasingly available.
- **West Crowley & the Rural Fringe**: Acreage properties with Benbrook Lake access; mixed housing ages from older rural homes to newer custom builds.
- **North of Main Street (established neighborhoods)**: 1950s–1980s ranch homes on larger lots with mature shade trees and walkable streets — a strong zone for full reroof and architectural-shingle upgrade conversations, with tree-canopy debris adding to wear.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Crowley Independent School District — 26 schools total, including 15 elementary schools, a Montessori Academy, four middle schools, two ninth-grade schools, two high schools, an alternative learning center, and a career and technology education center. Crowley ISD enrollment has more than doubled in the last 15 years, driven substantially by Chisholm Trail Parkway-corridor growth.
- **Infrastructure landmark**: The Chisholm Trail Parkway itself is a defining local landmark, with 9.7 miles and 18 interchanges running through the ISD's boundaries.
- **Recreation**: Benbrook Lake access from the city's western/rural fringe.
- **Civic anchor**: Crowley Area Chamber of Commerce, 201 N. Hampton Rd., Crowley, TX 76036.

## 4. History

- The area now occupied by Crowley was first settled in 1848; the community's name honors Colonel Benjamin Franklin Crowley.
- Early growth was modest — the town had fewer than 300 residents in the late 1920s, reaching roughly 500 by the end of World War II.
- An incorporation election was held on February 3, 1951, establishing Crowley as an incorporated town; the town council voted to redesignate Crowley from a town to a city on September 3, 1972.
- Population growth remained steady through the late 20th century (6,990 in 1990, 7,449 in 2000) before accelerating sharply in the 2000s–2010s (12,837 by 2010, 18,059+ by 2020), driven largely by the construction of the Chisholm Trail Parkway, which runs extensively through Crowley ISD and opened the area to major new residential and commercial development.
- Crowley ISD enrollment has more than doubled over the past 15 years, with continued growth expected as Parkway-corridor development continues.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate — hot summers, mild winters, with the North Texas region's characteristic spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Tarrant County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data). Crowley shares full exposure to the DFW hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Straight-line thunderstorm winds are common and frequently cause shingle lift/blow-off independent of hail; West Crowley's open rural fringe sees more direct wind exposure than denser, tree-covered neighborhoods.
- **Roofing material impact**: Crowley's rapid growth means a large and growing share of newer homes already carry architectural shingle from the builder; the deeper, older North Main Street-area housing stock is the strongest near-term market for full reroofs and Class 4 upgrades.

## 6. Roofing & Construction Industry Relevance

- **Materials by era**: North Crowley's 1950s–1980s ranch homes are largely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle. Deer Creek Estates/Creekside (1990s–2010s) are a mix of 3-tab and architectural shingle. FM 1187 corridor new construction is predominantly builder-grade architectural shingle with rising Class 4 adoption.
- **Replacement/upgrade sweet spot**: The older North Main Street neighborhoods represent the deepest "due for reroof" segment, while the fast-growing FM 1187 corridor represents the strongest new-construction material-upgrade conversation.
- **Insurance claim patterns**: Tarrant County's top-ten ranking for wind/hail claims applies directly; Crowley's rapid rooftop growth means claim volume is likely rising in step with its expanding housing stock.
- **HOA considerations**: Deer Creek Estates, Creekside, and FM 1187-corridor subdivisions commonly carry HOA architectural review requirements — confirm CC&Rs on shingle type/color before quoting upgrades. West Crowley's rural fringe is generally non-HOA.
- **Permit authority**: City of Crowley Community Development Department (Building Permits & Inspections) — phone **(817) 297-2201 ext. 3000**.
- **Code requirements**: Texas has no mandatory statewide residential building code; the state adopted the IRC as a default standard, but each incorporated city enforces its own adopted edition. Crowley, as an incorporated city, administers its own building permit and inspection requirements — confirm current code edition with Community Development before scoping work.
