# Venus, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Johnson and Ellis Counties (straddles the county line, with the original townsite in the northeastern corner of Johnson County)
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 15 miles east-southeast of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base; roughly 30 miles south of downtown Fort Worth and 30 miles southwest of downtown Dallas, sitting at the junction once served by two major railroads
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: U.S. Highway 67, FM 157
- **Population**: 2024 estimates vary by source (roughly 6,745–8,209); 2020 Census population was 4,361 — one of the fastest-growing small towns in the Kingdom Care service radius. Historically: 977 (1990), 910 (2000), and 2,960 (2010) — a 225.3% increase between 2000 and 2010 alone, driven by the southward expansion of the DFW Metroplex
- **Incorporation**: Incorporated in 1903; founded by J.C. Smythe, who purchased 80 acres of abandoned cornfields in the 1880s and named the new townsite "Venus" after the daughter of a local doctor; by 1888 the town had a post office and roughly 10 residents, growing quickly once it sat at the junction of two railroads
- **General character**: A small but rapidly growing town positioned between Dallas and Fort Worth, transitioning from a sleepy rural railroad town into an active rooftop-growth market as DFW suburban development pushes south along US 67.

## 4. History

- Venus traces its founding to the 1880s, when J.C. Smythe purchased 80 acres of abandoned cornfields in the northeastern corner of Johnson County and began planning a townsite, naming it after the daughter of a local doctor.
- By 1888 the new town had a post office and about 10 residents; sitting at the junction of two major railroads, Venus became for a time one of the fastest-growing communities in Johnson County.
- The city formally incorporated in 1903, consolidating its early railroad-era growth.
- Population growth was slow and steady through most of the 20th century (977 in 1990, actually dipping slightly to 910 by 2000), before the DFW Metroplex's southward suburban expansion triggered explosive growth after 2000 — a 225.3% increase to 2,960 by 2010, followed by continued strong growth to 4,361 (2020 Census) and an estimated 6,700–8,200 by 2024.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate typical of North Texas — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Johnson and Ellis Counties both fall within the broader North Texas hail corridor, sharing exposure to major regional events including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW/North Texas region has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952, with Venus's position along the US 67 growth corridor placing it within the same spring severe-weather risk band as the rest of the metro.
- **Wind**: Venus's more open, semi-rural terrain on the town's edges offers less windbreak than denser suburban areas, meaning straight-line thunderstorm winds can have a more direct impact on roofing than in tree-covered neighborhoods.
- **Roofing material impact**: Venus's rapid growth plus full regional hail exposure makes Class 4 impact-resistant shingle a strong fit both for new-construction specification and for storm-driven replacement on the town's older in-town homes.
