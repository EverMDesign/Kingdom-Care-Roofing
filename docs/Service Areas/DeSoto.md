# DeSoto, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Dallas County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 30 miles northeast of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base (roughly 45–55 minutes by car)
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Interstate 35E, U.S. Highway 67
- **Population**: Estimated 56,711 (2024); grew from approximately 400 at incorporation (1949) to 30,538 (1990), 37,800 (2000), 49,056 (2010), and 56,108 (2020) — strong, sustained growth over seven decades
- **Incorporation**: Settled in 1847, one of the oldest communities in North Texas; a post office was established in 1881, and the town was named for Dr. Thomas Hernando DeSoto Stewart; incorporated March 3, 1949 (following a petition by 42 eligible voters), becoming the nineteenth organized municipality in Dallas County
- **General character**: A well-established southern Dallas County suburb and part of the "Best Southwest" partnership (with Cedar Hill, Duncanville, and Lancaster), known for strong schools, mature residential neighborhoods, and active community programming.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Hidden Creek**: Spacious homes with easy access to community parks like DeSoto Town Center Park; housing stock generally mid-to-late 20th century, a strong candidate zone for architectural shingle upgrades.
- **Northern DeSoto neighborhoods (near Southwest Collegiate Institute for the Deaf)**: Established residential areas with housing spanning several decades.
- **Areas along the DeSoto Hike and Bike Trail**: Residential neighborhoods with scenic, tree-lined streets — mature canopy adds to roofing wear considerations.
- **General housing stock**: Given DeSoto's growth curve (tripling in population from 1990 to today), the city carries a deep mix of 1970s–1990s legacy homes alongside 2000s–2020s newer construction, giving a wide range of 3-tab, architectural, and increasingly Class 4 shingle inventory.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: DeSoto Independent School District — 12 schools (seven elementary, three middle schools, a high school, and a freshman campus), serving roughly 8,700 students across DeSoto, Glenn Heights, and Ovilla; mascot is the Eagle. Curtistene S. McCowan Middle School opened in 2007 as part of a district reorganization from two junior highs into a middle-school model.
- **Education**: Southwest Collegiate Institute for the Deaf, located near the city's northern neighborhoods.
- **Parks/Recreation**: DeSoto Town Center Park, DeSoto Hike and Bike Trail.
- **Community traditions**: Annual DeSoto Freedom Run through various neighborhoods.

## 4. History

- The DeSoto area was first settled in 1847, making it one of the oldest communities in North Texas.
- A post office was established in 1881, and the town took its name in honor of Dr. Thomas Hernando DeSoto Stewart, a physician dedicated to the community.
- On February 17, 1949, a petition signed by 42 eligible voters was presented to the Dallas County Judge requesting an incorporation election; the results were certified on March 3, 1949, officially creating the City of DeSoto with an estimated population of about 400 — the nineteenth organized municipality in Dallas County.
- The city grew modestly through the mid-20th century before entering a sustained growth phase from the 1990s onward, nearly doubling from 30,538 (1990) to over 56,000 by the mid-2020s.
- DeSoto ISD has expanded and reorganized over the decades, including the 2007 opening of Curtistene S. McCowan Middle School and the conversion of the district's two junior highs into a middle-school structure.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW metro — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Dallas County is a historically high-frequency hail county within the DFW region, sharing full exposure to major regional events including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history and underscores risk extending into late December.
- **Wind**: Mature tree canopy along the Hike and Bike Trail corridor and in established neighborhoods like Hidden Creek adds debris-related roof damage risk during high-wind events.
- **Roofing material impact**: DeSoto's deep stock of 1990s–2000s housing is now well into typical reroof age, making Class 4 impact-resistant shingle upgrades a strong, timely conversation at both scheduled replacement and claim-driven reroof points.

## 6. Roofing & Construction Industry Relevance

- **Materials by era**: Older DeSoto neighborhoods (1970s–1990s) are largely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle; 1990s–2000s growth-era homes (including Hidden Creek) are a mix of 3-tab and architectural shingle; newer 2010s–2020s construction trends toward builder-grade architectural shingle with rising Class 4 adoption.
- **Replacement/upgrade sweet spot**: With the bulk of DeSoto's housing stock dating to its major 1990s–2010s growth wave, a large share of homes are now in the 15–35-year reroof window — a deep, active replacement market.
- **Insurance claim patterns**: Dallas County's consistently high hail-claim frequency applies directly to DeSoto; claim-driven replacement activity follows the same regional storm cadence as the broader DFW metro.
- **HOA considerations**: Newer DeSoto subdivisions may carry HOA architectural review requirements; confirm CC&Rs on shingle type/color before quoting upgrades in planned-community areas.
- **Permit authority**: City of DeSoto Building Inspections Department, 211 E. Pleasant Run Rd., DeSoto, TX 75115 — phone **(972) 230-9610**.
- **Code requirements**: Texas has no mandatory statewide residential building code; the state adopted the IRC as a default standard, but each incorporated city enforces its own adopted edition. DeSoto, as an incorporated city, administers its own building permit and inspection requirements — confirm current code edition with Building Inspections before scoping work.
