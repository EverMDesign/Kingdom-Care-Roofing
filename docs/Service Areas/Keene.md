# Keene, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Johnson County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 9–15 miles south of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base (sources vary; Keene sits in central Johnson County, bordered to the west by Cleburne, the county seat)

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Keene Independent School District (3625 US-67 #C) — serves Pre-K through 12 with one elementary school, one middle school, one high school (Wanda R. Smith High School, established 1989, at 404 Charger Drive), and one alternative learning center.
- **Higher education**: Southwestern Adventist University — founded in 1893 as Keene Industrial Academy, the only four-year institution of higher learning in Johnson County.
- **Historic landmark**: The petrified wood "Mizpah Gate," the historic entrance to the college campus.
- **Historic trail significance**: Keene sits directly along the historic Chisholm Trail, used in the late 1800s to drive cattle from Texas to Kansas railheads.
- **Civic**: Keene Municipal Building, 100 N. Mockingbird Ln., Keene, TX 76059.

## 4. History

- Keene was founded in 1893–1894 as a Seventh-day Adventist community built specifically around education, anchored by the establishment of the Texas School — which became Keene Industrial Academy and ultimately Southwestern Adventist University.
- The town developed as a recognized Adventist "company town," with institutional and residential growth closely tied to the university's presence; by the mid-1940s, Keene had grown to 350 residents and sixteen businesses.
- Population growth accelerated through the late 20th century, climbing from 4,302 residents in 1990 to over 6,400 by 2020, as the city expanded beyond its original institutional core.
- Keene's position directly on the historic Chisholm Trail adds a layer of 19th-century cattle-drive heritage to its Adventist educational identity.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW/North Texas region — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Johnson County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data); Keene shares full exposure to the regional hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Straight-line thunderstorm winds are common in spring and frequently cause shingle lift/blow-off independent of hail.
- **Roofing material impact**: Keene's mix of century-old institutional-core housing and steadier 20th-century residential growth makes it a solid market for both legacy full-reroof work and Class 4 impact-resistant upgrades at next replacement.
