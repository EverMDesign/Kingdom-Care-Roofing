# Cedar Hill, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Dallas County (a small portion extends into Ellis County)
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 21 miles northeast of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base (straight-line distance; roughly 33 miles via typical driving routes)
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: U.S. Highway 67, State Highway 342 (Belt Line Road), FM 1382
- **Population**: Approximately 49,148 (2020 Census); grew from 500 (1890) to 476 (1940), 732 (1950), 1,848 (1960), 2,610 (1970), 6,849 (1980), 19,988 (1990), 32,093 (2000), and 45,028 (2010) — explosive growth especially after 1989, when nearby Joe Pool Lake opened
- **Incorporation**: Incorporated in 1938; Cedar Hill is the oldest organized community in Dallas County and once served as the county's temporary seat, lying along a branch of the historic Chisholm Trail
- **General character**: A well-established, scenic southwest Dallas County suburb known for its hilly terrain, mature neighborhoods, and lakefront recreation around Joe Pool Lake and Cedar Hill State Park.

---

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Lake Ridge**: Known for expansive lots, mature trees, and proximity to Joe Pool Lake; housing here tends toward larger, established homes with a meaningful share of roofs now due for a second or third reroof cycle.
- **Older in-town areas near historic Cedar Hill (original 1938-incorporation core)**: Some of the oldest housing stock in the city, with layered generations of asphalt shingle replacement.
- **Post-1989 lake-boom subdivisions**: Built out rapidly following the 1989/1991 opening of Joe Pool Lake and Cedar Hill State Park, these neighborhoods span the 1990s–2000s and commonly carry architectural shingle.
- **Newer development near U.S. 67 and Belt Line Road corridors**: More recent construction trending toward builder-grade architectural shingle with growing Class 4 impact-resistant adoption.

---

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Cedar Hill Independent School District, plus nearby charter school options.
- **Parks/Recreation**: Cedar Hill State Park (1,826 acres on the 7,500-acre Joe Pool Lake; acquired by the state in 1982, opened 1991) — offers hiking, biking, picnicking, camping, boating, and fishing; the park sits at the intersection of the Cross Timbers and Blackland Prairie ecoregions and hosts over 200 bird species.
- **Historic/Cultural**: Penn Farm Agricultural History Center within Cedar Hill State Park, preserving remnants of the original Penn family farm as a tribute to the disappearing Texas family farm; Dogwood Canyon Audubon Center (hiking, boating, wildlife observation).
- **General character landmark**: Cedar Hill's hilly, wooded terrain (among the most topographically distinct in the immediate Dallas area) is itself a recognizable local feature.

---

## 4. History

- Cedar Hill is the oldest organized community in Dallas County, with settlement dating to the area's early frontier period along a branch of the Chisholm Trail; the community briefly served as the temporary county seat of Dallas County.
- The city incorporated in 1938, formalizing a slow-growing agricultural community that remained under 1,000 residents through the 1940s.
- Growth was gradual through the mid-20th century (732 in 1950 to 2,610 in 1970) before accelerating sharply in the 1980s (6,849 by 1980, nearly 20,000 by 1990).
- The 1982 state acquisition of land for Cedar Hill State Park and the 1989 opening of Joe Pool Lake (with the park opening in 1991) triggered a major residential growth wave, as lakefront and near-lake development drove the population from roughly 20,000 in 1990 to over 49,000 by 2020.
- Continued suburban build-out along the U.S. 67 and Belt Line Road corridors has sustained growth into the 2020s.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW metro — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Dallas County is a historically high-frequency hail county within the DFW region, sharing full exposure to major regional events including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history and illustrates risk extending into late December.
- **Wind**: Cedar Hill's elevated, hilly terrain and lakefront exposure around Joe Pool Lake can amplify straight-line wind effects during spring storm season, adding to shingle-lift and blow-off risk.
- **Roofing material impact**: The combination of regional hail intensity, elevated terrain wind exposure, and lakefront siting makes Class 4 impact-resistant shingle upgrades particularly relevant for Cedar Hill homeowners, especially in Lake Ridge and other lake-adjacent neighborhoods.

## 6. Roofing & Construction Industry Relevance

- **Materials by era**: Older in-town housing near the historic 1938-incorporation core is largely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle. Post-1989 lake-boom subdivisions (1990s–2000s) are a mix of 3-tab and architectural shingle. Newer development near U.S. 67/Belt Line Road trends toward builder-grade architectural shingle with rising Class 4 adoption.
- **Replacement/upgrade sweet spot**: The large wave of 1990s–2000s lake-boom construction is now well into its 20–35-year window — a deep, mature reroof market, concentrated in and around Lake Ridge and similar established subdivisions.
- **Insurance claim patterns**: Dallas County's consistently high hail-claim frequency applies directly to Cedar Hill; claim-driven replacement activity follows the same regional storm cadence as the broader DFW metro.
- **HOA considerations**: Many of Cedar Hill's lake-area and newer subdivisions are HOA-governed with architectural review requirements — confirm CC&Rs on shingle type/color before quoting upgrades.
- **Permit authority**: City of Cedar Hill Building Inspections Department — phone **(972) 291-5100 ext. 1090**; email citypermits@cedarhilltx.com.
- **Code requirements**: Texas has no mandatory statewide residential building code; the state adopted the IRC as a default standard, but each incorporated city enforces its own adopted edition. Cedar Hill, as an incorporated city, administers its own building permit and inspection requirements — confirm current code edition with the Building Inspections Department before scoping work.
