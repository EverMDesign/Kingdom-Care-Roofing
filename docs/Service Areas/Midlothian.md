# Midlothian, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Ellis County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 20 miles east-southeast of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base (roughly 25 miles southwest of Dallas)
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: U.S. Highway 287, State Highway 342
- **Population**: 2024 estimate 44,104; grew from 9,258 (1990) to 13,210 (2000), 22,975 (2010), and 35,134 (2020) — population has nearly doubled between 2010 and 2024, making Midlothian one of the fastest-growing cities in North Texas
- **Incorporation**: Incorporated in 1888, following the arrival of the Gulf, Colorado & Santa Fe Railroad (1883) and the Houston & Texas Central Railroad (1886); though a few Anglo settlers arrived as early as 1847, formal town establishment followed the railroads' arrival
- **General character**: A rapidly growing Ellis County city known historically as the "Cement Capital of Texas" due to its limestone deposits and major cement manufacturing presence, now blending that industrial legacy with fast-growing master-planned suburban development.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Downtown Midlothian**: Historic core tied to the city's 1888 incorporation and railroad-era origins; oldest housing stock in the city, likely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle.
- **Longbranch area**: Established neighborhood with a mix of housing eras.
- **Somercrest and BridgeWater**: Newer master-planned communities reflecting Midlothian's recent explosive growth; predominantly architectural (dimensional) shingle, with Class 4 impact-resistant options increasingly specified.
- **Near cement industry corridor (Cement Valley Rd and surrounding areas)**: Reflects the city's industrial heritage; a mix of older and newer residential development nearby.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Midlothian Independent School District — covers nearly 112 square miles with nine school facilities serving more than 7,000 students (16:1 student-teacher ratio); ranked the #1 district in Ellis County by Niche, with 85% of campuses earning A or B TEA ratings.
- **Industrial/Historic**: Midlothian is home to three of the top ten largest cement factories in the United States — Texas Industries ("TXI"), Holcim, and Ash Grove — a legacy of the city's limestone deposits along the Austin Chalk Escarpment, earning it the nickname "Cement Capital of Texas."
- **Parks/Recreation**: More than a dozen scenic parks and recreational spaces citywide, including Heritage Park; golf courses in the surrounding area include BlackHorse Golf Club, Cypress Lakes, Timber Creek Golf Club, and Legendary Oaks Golf Course.

## 4. History

- While a few Anglo settlers reached the Midlothian area as early as 1847, the town itself was not formally established until two railroads crossed the area — the Gulf, Colorado & Santa Fe in 1883 and the Houston & Texas Central in 1886.
- The city incorporated in 1888, shortly after the railroads' arrival spurred local settlement and commerce.
- The discovery of substantial limestone deposits along the Austin Chalk Escarpment proved pivotal, attracting major cement manufacturers and earning Midlothian its nickname, the "Cement Capital of Texas" — a legacy still reflected today in three of the nation's largest cement factories operating within the city.
- Population growth was modest through the late 20th century (9,258 in 1990) before accelerating dramatically in the 21st century — more than tripling from 13,210 (2000) to over 44,000 by 2024, driven by master-planned suburban development spreading south from the Dallas metro.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW metro — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Ellis County shares exposure to the broader North Texas hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW/North Texas region has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952 in the immediate metro, with the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remaining the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Straight-line thunderstorm winds are common in spring and frequently cause shingle lift/blow-off independent of hail, affecting both Midlothian's historic core and its rapidly expanding newer subdivisions.
- **Roofing material impact**: Midlothian's explosive growth means a large and growing share of newer homes already carry architectural shingle from the builder; the historic downtown core and established neighborhoods like Longbranch represent the strongest near-term market for full reroofs and Class 4 upgrades.
