# Burleson, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Primarily Johnson County, with a small portion extending north into Tarrant County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: This is Kingdom Care's home base city (1133 Cardinal Ridge Road, Burleson, TX 76028)
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Interstate 35W, Chisholm Trail Parkway, U.S. Highway 174, FM 1187
- **Population**: 2024 estimate approximately 57,625 (Census estimate); grew from 16,113 (1990) to 20,976 (2000), 36,690 (2010), and 48,132 (2020) — nearly tripling since 2000, among the fastest-growing cities in the Kingdom Care service radius
- **Incorporation**: Settled in 1881 with the arrival of the Missouri-Kansas-Texas railroad; railroad representative Grenville M. Dodge purchased land from Rev. Henry C. Renfro for the depot and townsite at what is now "Old Town" Burleson
- **General character**: A fast-growing Fort Worth-area suburb anchored by a preserved historic downtown ("Old Town") and expanding rapidly along the Chisholm Trail Parkway corridor with new master-planned residential development.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Old Town Burleson**: Historic commercial and civic core centered along Ellison Street; home to the city's oldest housing stock (much pre-dating mid-century), farmers markets, Mayor Vera Calvin Plaza live music, and a growing restaurant/retail scene in preserved historic facades. Older homes here are strong candidates for full tear-off reroofs, with some original wood-frame structures.
- **Southwest Burleson established family neighborhoods**: Good access to Burleson ISD schools, parks, and the Chisholm Trail Parkway; subdivisions typically feature single-family homes on generous lots, many with active HOAs — housing here spans the 1990s–2010s with a strong share of architectural shingle.
- **Newer master-planned communities on the city's expanding edges**: The fastest-growing part of Burleson, built out over the last decade-plus along the Chisholm Trail Parkway corridor; new construction here is predominantly architectural (dimensional) shingle, with increasing Class 4 impact-resistant adoption.
- **Renfro-Clark area / historic home district**: Tied to the Renfro family's founding-era land, reflecting some of the city's earliest residential development.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Burleson Independent School District (BISD) — 3 high schools, 2 middle schools, and 10 elementary schools, with several campuses rated Recognized or Exemplary by the Texas Education Agency.
- **Historic/Cultural**: Old Town Burleson (Ellison Street district), the Renfro-Clark historic home, the Russell Farm Art Center (hosts guided history tours, art classes, and community events including "Night of Lights" and "Christmas on the Farm" in December).
- **Civic/Recreation**: Burleson Public Library, community center, a 25-meter public pool, Mayor Vera Calvin Plaza.
- **Commercial corridor**: Development along the Chisholm Trail Parkway and I-35W frontage.

## 4. History

- Burleson traces its founding to 1881, when the Missouri-Kansas-Texas railroad arrived and company representative Grenville M. Dodge purchased land from Rev. Henry C. Renfro for a depot and townsite — the origin of today's Old Town Burleson.
- By the close of the 19th century, Burleson was a bustling rural town with farms, schools, a grocery store, cotton gins, grist mills, a general store, a druggist, a bank, and a jeweler.
- Growth remained modest through most of the 20th century, with the population only reaching 16,113 by 1990.
- Explosive growth began in the 1990s–2000s and accelerated sharply after 2010 (36,690 in 2010 to 48,132 in 2020, and an estimated 57,625 by 2024), driven largely by the opening and extension of the Chisholm Trail Parkway, which connected Burleson directly into the Fort Worth commuter corridor and spurred major new residential and commercial development on the city's edges.
- The city has invested significantly in preserving and revitalizing Old Town Burleson, maintaining historic facades while filling them with new breweries, boutiques, and restaurants even as the city's overall footprint has expanded rapidly.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate — hot summers, mild winters, and a pronounced spring/early-summer severe weather season typical of North Texas.
- **Hail risk**: Johnson County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data). Burleson shares full exposure to the DFW hail corridor, including the record-setting 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses, with a March 23, 2016 storm alone causing over $1 billion in DFW-area damage).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952. The December 26, 2015 outbreak (Garland–Rowlett EF4, $1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history, underscoring risk extending into late December as well as peak spring months.
- **Wind**: Straight-line thunderstorm winds are common in spring and frequently cause shingle lift/blow-off independent of hail.
- **Roofing material impact**: Burleson's rapid growth means a large and growing share of newer homes already carry architectural shingle from the builder; the deep, denser Old Town and established-neighborhood housing stock represents the strongest near-term market for Class 4 impact-resistant upgrades at next replacement or claim event.

## 6. Roofing & Construction Industry Relevance

- **Materials by era**: Old Town Burleson's oldest homes are likely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle, with some original wood-frame construction. 1990s–2010s southwest Burleson neighborhoods are a mix of 3-tab and architectural shingle. New construction along the Chisholm Trail Parkway corridor is predominantly builder-grade architectural shingle, with Class 4 impact-resistant options increasingly specified.
- **Replacement/upgrade sweet spot**: With such rapid population growth since 2000, Burleson has a large volume of homes now entering the 15–25-year reroof window — a strong, growing core replacement market, supplemented by claim-driven reroofs after regional storm events.
- **Insurance claim patterns**: Johnson County's top-ten ranking for wind/hail claims applies directly to Burleson; claim volume and restoration activity track the same regional storm cadence as the rest of the DFW metro.
- **HOA considerations**: Many of Burleson's newer subdivisions (southwest Burleson and the Chisholm Trail Parkway corridor) are HOA-governed with active architectural review committees — confirm CC&Rs on shingle color/type before quoting upgrades.
- **Permit authority**: City of Burleson Development Services Department — phone **(817) 426-9611**; Building Permits and Inspections Office — phone **(817) 426-9632**; main city line **(817) 426-9600**.
- **Code requirements**: Texas has no mandatory statewide residential building code; the state adopted the IRC as a default standard, but each incorporated city enforces its own adopted edition. Burleson, as an incorporated city, administers its own building permit and inspection requirements — confirm current code edition and wind-speed provisions with Development Services before scoping work.
