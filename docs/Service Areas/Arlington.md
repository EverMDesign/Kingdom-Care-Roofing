# Arlington, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Tarrant County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 20–25 miles northeast of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base (roughly 22.5 miles via I-20 W and I-35 S)
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Interstate 20, Interstate 30, State Highway 360, U.S. Highway 287
- **Population**: Approximately 400,000–414,000 (2025 NCTCOG estimate: 413,955); grew from 160,113 (1980) to 261,721 (1990), 332,969 (2000), 365,438 (2010), and 394,266 (2020) — a 60%+ increase since 2000, making Arlington one of the largest cities in Tarrant County and the DFW metro
- **Incorporation**: Incorporated in 1884; transformed from a sleepy agricultural village into a major city largely through the efforts of longtime mayor Tom Vandergriff, who brought the University of Texas at Arlington and Six Flags to the city
- **General character**: A major suburban anchor city within DFW, home to large-scale entertainment/sports infrastructure (AT&T Stadium, Globe Life Field, Six Flags), a large public university, and a mix of mature mid-century neighborhoods alongside newer master-planned development on the city's southern edge.

---

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Lake Arlington area (North Arlington)**: Larger, higher-priced homes with water access; housing stock generally mid-to-late 20th century, often now on second-generation architectural shingle roofs.
- **West Arlington / near Veterans Park, Richard Simpson Park, Rush Creek Linear Park, Lake Arlington Golf Course**: Established mid-century neighborhoods with a strong mature-tree canopy — a common driver of accelerated shingle wear and debris-related roof damage.
- **South Arlington (ZIP 76017 and surrounding)**: Family-friendly, mid-range housing; the southern edge of Arlington has been the city's primary new-construction zone for the past decade, with master-planned subdivisions, larger lots, and modern floor plans — strong architectural and Class 4 shingle penetration here.
- **Arlington Gardens, Wooded Acres, Toliver Acres, and similar older platted additions**: Smaller, older in-town neighborhoods with legacy housing stock likely to include original or early-generation 3-tab asphalt roofing still due for upgrade.
- **Entertainment District (near AT&T Stadium/Globe Life Field)**: Primarily commercial/mixed-use, not a residential roofing focus area but relevant for name recognition in local SEO.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Arlington Independent School District (AISD) — one of the largest districts in the state — plus a portion of the city served by other neighboring districts; Arlington ISD's Athletics Center and Career + Technical Center are active community hubs.
- **Higher education**: University of Texas at Arlington (UTA).
- **Sports/Entertainment**: AT&T Stadium (Dallas Cowboys), Globe Life Field (Texas Rangers, opened 2020, replacing the former Globe Life Park), Six Flags Over Texas (opened 1961, the first regional theme park in the U.S.), Six Flags Hurricane Harbor.
- **Entertainment District**: Texas Live!, International Bowling Museum & Hall of Fame, Live! by Loews hotel — all developed since 2009.
- **Parks/Recreation**: Veterans Park, Richard Simpson Park, Rush Creek Linear Park, River Legacy Park, Lake Arlington and its golf course.
- **Civic anchor**: Greater Arlington Chamber of Commerce, which partners with AISD on job fairs and youth entrepreneurship programs.

## 4. History

- Arlington was established in the 1870s along the Texas and Pacific Railway and incorporated in 1884, remaining a modest agricultural town for decades.
- The city's transformation into a major suburb accelerated after World War II, driven substantially by longtime mayor Tom Vandergriff, who successfully recruited the University of Texas at Arlington and the original Six Flags theme park (opened August 1961) to the city.
- Wet 'n Wild water park joined the Six Flags family in 1996, becoming Six Flags Hurricane Harbor — now the largest water park in the southwestern U.S.
- The 2009 opening of AT&T Stadium anchored a new Entertainment District that has since added Globe Life Field (2020, Texas Rangers' current home, replacing the adjacent former ballpark), Texas Live!, and associated hospitality development.
- Residential growth pushed steadily southward through the late 20th and early 21st centuries, with the southern part of the city emerging as Arlington's primary new-construction corridor over the past decade.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate — hot summers, mild winters, with the DFW metro's characteristic spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Tarrant County (including Arlington) is one of the DFW counties consistently ranked in the top ten for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data). Arlington experienced damaging hail during the record-setting 2016 North Texas hail season, which produced roughly 500,000 statewide hail claims and over $4 billion in insured losses, including a March 23, 2016 storm that caused over $1 billion in DFW-area damage.
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952. The December 26, 2015 outbreak (Garland–Rowlett EF4, 12 confirmed tornadoes, ~$1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in the metro's history and illustrates the region's risk window extending into late December as well as peak spring months.
- **Wind**: Straight-line thunderstorm winds are common and frequently cause shingle lifting/blow-off independent of hail impact.
- **Roofing material impact**: Arlington's dense rooftop count and high storm-claim frequency make it one of the most active insurance restoration markets in the DFW metro; Class 4 impact-resistant shingle upgrades are increasingly common at claim-driven replacement, both for durability and for the insurance premium discounts many Texas carriers offer.

## 6. Roofing & Construction Industry Relevance

- **Materials by era**: Mid-century North/West Arlington neighborhoods commonly carry multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle replacement; 1980s–2000s housing stock is a mix of 3-tab and early architectural shingle; South Arlington's last-decade master-planned construction is predominantly builder-grade architectural (dimensional) shingle, with some newer builds specifying Class 4 impact-resistant product upfront.
- **Replacement/upgrade sweet spot**: The large stock of 20–40-year-old homes across North and West Arlington represents a deep, steady reroof market; claim-driven replacements following hail events are a major secondary channel given the county's high claim frequency.
- **Insurance claim patterns**: Tarrant County is a top-ten Texas county for wind/hail claim volume; Arlington's size and housing density make it one of the highest-volume restoration markets within Kingdom Care's service area.
- **HOA considerations**: Many of Arlington's newer South Arlington subdivisions are HOA-governed with architectural review committees; HOA approval does not substitute for a city permit — both are required, and HOA CC&Rs should be checked before specifying shingle color/type changes.
- **Permit authority**: City of Arlington Development Services — phone **(817) 459-6502**; 24-hour inspection hotline **(817) 261-8817**. Roofing contractors must be registered with the City of Arlington to obtain a building permit, and a permit is required for roofing material/covering replacement.
- **Code requirements**: Texas has no mandatory statewide residential code; the state adopted the IRC as a default standard but each incorporated city enforces its own adopted edition. Arlington, as an incorporated city, has its own adopted building code — confirm current edition and any wind-speed design provisions with Development Services before scoping work.

## 8. Community Connection Points

Given the city's size, hyper-local neighborhood Nextdoor groups and AISD-specific community Facebook groups are more effective trust channels than city-wide messaging alone.
