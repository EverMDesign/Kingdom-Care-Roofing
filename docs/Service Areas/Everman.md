# Everman, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Tarrant County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 6.6 miles north of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Interstate 820, near the southern edge of Fort Worth
- **Population**: 6,154 (2020 Census), up slightly from 6,108 (2010); grew from 451 (1950) to 1,076 (1960), 4,570 (1970), 5,387 (1980), 5,672 (1990), and 5,836 (2000) — explosive growth between 1960 and 1970 (more than quadrupling), with much slower, steady growth since

- **General character**: A small, incorporated residential community on the southern edge of Fort Worth near I-820 — a mature, stable working-class suburb with a strong local school-athletics identity.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Core residential Everman (built out primarily in the 1960s–1970s boom)**: Given the city's explosive 1960–1970 population growth (1,076 to 4,570), the bulk of Everman's housing stock dates to this era — now well past the typical roofing replacement window, likely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle.
- **1980s–1990s infill**: Smaller pockets of later construction as the city's growth slowed; a mix of 3-tab and early architectural shingle.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Everman Independent School District — five elementary schools (K–4), two intermediate schools (grades 5–6), one junior high school, and two high schools, including Everman Joe C. Bean High School, home to the 2001 and 2002 Texas 3A state football championship teams and the Texas 3A state boys' basketball championship team.
- **Historic**: The Barron Munitions Building, which served as a schoolhouse for Everman after being used for munitions purposes; received a Texas Historical Marker in 1976 via the Everman Garden Club. Schools in the area were consolidated in 1906, leading to the eventual establishment of Everman ISD.
- **Civic**: Everman City Hall, 212 N. Race St., Everman, TX 76140.

## 4. History

- Everman incorporated in 1945 as a small residential community on Fort Worth's southern edge.
- Early growth was modest (451 residents in 1950), but the 1960s brought explosive expansion — the population more than quadrupled from 1,076 (1960) to 4,570 (1970), reflecting the broader southward suburban expansion of the Fort Worth metro during that decade.
- Growth slowed significantly after 1970, with the population essentially plateauing in the 5,300–6,200 range from 1980 through 2020 — indicating a city that reached its practical build-out capacity decades ago.
- Everman ISD traces its organizational roots to a 1906 school consolidation, and the district's Joe C. Bean High School became a source of significant local pride through back-to-back state championships in football (2001, 2002) and a state championship in boys' basketball.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW metro — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Tarrant County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data); Everman shares full exposure to the DFW hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Everman's mature, long-settled 1960s–1970s housing stock typically carries established tree canopy, adding debris-related roof damage risk during high-wind events.
- **Roofing material impact**: Given the city's older, largely built-out housing stock, the market here is heavily weighted toward full tear-off reroof and Class 4 impact-resistant upgrade conversations rather than new-construction specification.
