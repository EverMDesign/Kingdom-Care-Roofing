# Waxahachie, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Ellis County (county seat)
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 29.6 miles east of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base; also roughly 30 miles south of downtown Dallas via Interstate 35E
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Interstate 35E, U.S. Highway 287, U.S. Highway 77
- **Population**: 2024 estimate 48,617 (the largest city in Ellis County); 41,140 at the 2020 Census — population has nearly doubled since 2000, reflecting substantial DFW spillover growth
- **Incorporation**: Founded in August 1850 as the seat of newly established Ellis County, on land donated by early settler Emory W. Rogers; incorporated in 1871
- **General character**: A historic county-seat city renowned for its exceptionally well-preserved Victorian architecture — nicknamed "Gingerbread City" — combined with rapid recent suburban growth as exurban DFW expansion reaches into Ellis County.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Historic residential district surrounding the downtown square**: Contains one of the finest collections of Victorian, Queen Anne, and Craftsman architecture in North Texas, built largely during the construction boom of the late 1800s and early 1900s; this is the oldest and most architecturally significant housing stock in the Kingdom Care service area, likely carrying multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle (with some homes requiring specialized care given historic preservation considerations).
- **Five National Register of Historic Places districts**: Underscore the depth of historic residential and commercial development concentrated near downtown.
- **Newer DFW-spillover growth subdivisions**: Reflecting the near-doubling of population since 2000; predominantly builder-grade architectural (dimensional) shingle, with Class 4 impact-resistant options increasingly specified.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Waxahachie Independent School District — eight elementary campuses, three middle-school campuses, and two high schools, including Waxahachie Global High School (ranking in the top 10% of Texas schools, with a 99.1% on-time graduation rate for the Class of 2023).
- **Historic/Cultural**: The Romanesque Revival-style Ellis County Courthouse (1897), designed by architect J. Riely Gordon using red limestone and pink granite, featuring a nine-story clock tower and castle-like turrets — reputed to be the second-most photographed historic landmark in Texas after the Alamo. Five National Register of Historic Places districts are located within the city.
- **"Gingerbread City" identity**: Waxahachie's Victorian architectural embellishments give it this nickname; the Ellis County Museum hosts the annual Gingerbread Trail Tour of Homes each June.
- **Civic**: Waxahachie Convention & Visitors Bureau; Waxahachie Chamber of Commerce.

## 4. History

- Waxahachie was founded in August 1850 as the seat of newly created Ellis County, on a tract of land donated by early settler Emory W. Rogers.
- The city formally incorporated in 1871.
- A strong late-1800s to early-1900s economy fueled a major construction boom, resulting in the rapid development of downtown and the large Victorian residential neighborhoods that define the city's architectural identity today.
- The 1897 completion of the Romanesque Revival Ellis County Courthouse — designed by noted architect J. Riely Gordon — became the city's defining architectural landmark and remains one of the most photographed historic buildings in Texas.
- Waxahachie's deep Victorian architectural heritage earned it the "Gingerbread City" nickname, celebrated annually through the Gingerbread Trail Tour of Homes.
- Population growth remained relatively modest for much of the 20th century before accelerating sharply as DFW's southward exurban expansion reached Ellis County — the city's population has nearly doubled since 2000, reaching an estimated 48,617 by 2024.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW/North Texas region — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Ellis County shares exposure to the broader North Texas hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW/North Texas region has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952 in the immediate metro, with the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remaining the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Waxahachie's mature, tree-lined historic neighborhoods see elevated debris-related roof damage risk during high-wind events; newer growth subdivisions see storm patterns typical of the broader DFW exurban corridor.
- **Roofing material impact**: Waxahachie's unique combination of historically significant Victorian-era homes and rapid new suburban growth creates two distinct roofing conversations — careful, preservation-minded reroofing in the historic core, and Class 4 impact-resistant material specification in new construction.
