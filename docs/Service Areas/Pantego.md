# Pantego, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Tarrant County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 20 miles northeast of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base; sits about 10 miles southeast of downtown Fort Worth and roughly 20 miles west of downtown Dallas, within the dense Arlington urban core
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Interstate 20, Cooper Street (SH 157), South Bowen Road
- **Population**: 2024 estimate approximately 2,337 — a small, stable, built-out community surrounded almost entirely by the City of Arlington
- **Incorporation**: First incorporated June 1949 (to prevent threatened annexation by Arlington), briefly dis-incorporated February 1952, then re-incorporated in its present form on May 22, 1952
- **General character**: A tiny, affluent, fully built-out town completely enclosed within Arlington and bordering Dalworthington Gardens — a "town within a city" with its own local government, tight-knit neighborhood feel, and no room for new large-scale development.

---

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Shady Valley Acres**: Established single-family subdivision reflecting Pantego's mid-20th-century build-out era; homes here are strong candidates for full roof replacement given their age.
- **South Bowen Road corridor homes**: Established residential streets near Town Hall, generally mid-century to late-20th-century construction.
- **Given Pantego's small size (roughly 1.9 square miles) and status as a fully built-out enclave town**, there has been no meaningful new subdivision development in recent decades — nearly all housing stock dates from the town's mid-20th-century incorporation era through the 1980s–1990s, making it almost entirely a legacy-reroof market rather than a new-construction market.

---

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Parks/Recreation**: Bicentennial Park — walking trails, sports fields, and playgrounds; the town's primary recreational anchor.
- **Schools**: Pantego sits within the Arlington Independent School District; nearby schools include Hill Elementary School and Bailey Junior High, feeding into Arlington High School. Pantego Christian Academy (private, with a well-known Mansfield campus) also carries the town's name.
- **Proximity landmarks**: Located less than 10 miles from AT&T Stadium (Dallas Cowboys), Globe Life Field (Texas Rangers), Texas Live!, Six Flags Over Texas, and Hurricane Harbor — all just across the Arlington line.
- **Shopping/commercial**: Cooper Street corridor and nearby Arlington Highlands and The Parks Mall at Arlington provide the town's practical shopping and dining access, since Pantego itself has minimal commercial footprint.
- **Civic**: Pantego Town Hall on South Bowen Road.

---

## 4. History

- Settlement of the Pantego area traces to the 1840s Peters Colony land grants in what became Tarrant County, with Colonel Middleton Tate Johnson and later Colonel Foscue acquiring land in the area after the Mexican-American War and Civil War.
- The town incorporated for the first time in June 1949 specifically to resist a threatened complete annexation by the rapidly growing City of Arlington.
- A brief dis-incorporation followed in February 1952, after which the town re-incorporated in its current, lasting form on May 22, 1952.
- Since then, Pantego has remained a small, stable, fully enclosed municipality within Arlington's urban fabric — its infrastructure and housing stock largely built out by the mid-to-late 20th century, with population holding steady in the low thousands rather than expanding.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate typical of the DFW metro core — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Tarrant County is a core part of the DFW hail corridor, fully exposed to major regional events including the record-setting 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses, with the March 23, 2016 storm alone causing over $1 billion in DFW-area damage).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; Pantego's dense urban location places it within the same risk band as central Arlington and Fort Worth, including exposure to outbreak events like December 26, 2015.
- **Wind**: Straight-line thunderstorm winds common in spring frequently cause shingle lift and blow-off in this densely built, mature-tree-canopy part of the metro.
- **Roofing material impact**: Pantego's aging, fully built-out housing stock combined with full DFW-core hail exposure makes it a strong market for Class 4 impact-resistant shingle upgrades at both routine replacement and storm-claim events.

## 6. Roofing & Construction Industry Relevance

- **Materials by era**: With virtually all homes dating from the 1950s–1990s, Pantego's roofing stock is overwhelmingly legacy 3-tab asphalt shingle, much of it now on multiple replacement cycles; architectural shingle appears mainly where prior reroofs have already occurred.
- **Replacement/upgrade sweet spot**: As a built-out, aging-housing-stock town with no new construction, Pantego represents a small but steady legacy-reroof and storm-claim market rather than a growth market — ideal territory for full-replacement and Class 4 upgrade conversations.
- **Insurance claim patterns**: Tarrant County's high claim density applies fully to Pantego; claim volume and restoration activity track the same regional storm cadence as central Arlington and Fort Worth.
- **HOA considerations**: No significant HOA-governed subdivisions were identified in Pantego; exterior/roofing decisions are governed by the town's own building and code enforcement rather than private HOA covenants.

## 8. Community Connection Points

- **Civic organization**: No independently confirmed dedicated Pantego chamber of commerce; the Town's Community Relations Board (CRB) functions as the civic/events body, meeting the first Tuesday of each month, 6:00 p.m., open to the public.
- **Annual events**: "Eggcellent" Easter, Movies in the Park (June–August), First Responder Appreciation Weeks, National Night Out, Halloween Trunk-or-Treat, and "A Pantego Christmas" featuring Santa's Candy Cane Parade — all run through the CRB.
- **Trust-building angles**: Pantego's small, close-knit "town within a city" identity means visibility at town-run events (Movies in the Park, Trunk-or-Treat, the Christmas parade) and direct relationships with Town Hall and the CRB carry outsized trust value relative to the town's small population; residents' strong sense of local distinctiveness from surrounding Arlington makes "we know Pantego specifically" messaging more effective than generic Arlington-area marketing.
