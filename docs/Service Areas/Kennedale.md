# Kennedale, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Tarrant County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 9 miles northeast of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Adjacent to Arlington and Mansfield in southeast Tarrant County; served by nearby State Highway 287 and FM 1187
- **Population**: Estimated 10,771 (July 2024); grew from 5,132 (1990) to 5,869 (2000), 6,745 (2010), and 8,518 (2020) — Kennedale is now among the fastest-growing cities in Tarrant County
- **Incorporation**: Established in 1882 at the site of a mineral well; the Town of Kennedale formally incorporated in July 1947 following an election, with a population of just 300 at the time (still only 325 in the late 1940s, growing to 500 by 1950)
- **General character**: A first-tier Fort Worth suburb adjacent to Arlington and Mansfield, transformed from a small mineral-well town into one of Tarrant County's fastest-growing residential communities.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Historic in-town core (near the original 1882 mineral well site and brick-manufacturing heritage)**: Oldest housing stock in the city, including homes tied to Kennedale's early 20th-century brick-manufacturing identity; likely multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle.
- **Mid-century growth neighborhoods (1950s–1990s)**: Built out as the city grew from 500 residents in 1950 to over 5,000 by 1990; a mix of 3-tab and early architectural shingle.
- **Recent rapid-growth subdivisions (driving growth from 6,745 in 2010 to an estimated 10,771 by 2024)**: New construction predominantly builder-grade architectural shingle, with Class 4 impact-resistant options increasingly specified.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Kennedale Independent School District — established around 1890; the district's second school building (1913) was constructed of brick specifically to reflect the town's brick-manufacturing heritage.
- **Historic/Cultural**: A Depression-era rock wall near the district's historic campus, attributed by oral history to the WPA, remains a recognized remnant of Tarrant County's New Deal-era infrastructure. The Kennedale Historical Society holds community historical materials dating from the early 1900s to 2022.
- **Civic**: Kennedale City Hall, 405 Municipal Dr., Kennedale, TX 76060.

## 4. History

- Kennedale was established in 1882 at the site of a mineral well, giving the early settlement its initial draw.
- The town grew slowly for decades — still just 325 residents in the late 1940s — before formally incorporating in July 1947 following a local election, with an incorporation-era population of about 300.
- The city's early 20th-century identity was closely tied to brick manufacturing, reflected in the 1913 construction of a new two-story brick school building specifically chosen to showcase this local industry.
- Population growth remained modest through the mid-20th century (500 by 1950) before accelerating steadily from the 1990s onward (5,132 in 1990 to 8,518 by 2020), and then sharply in the 2020s, with Kennedale now recognized as one of Tarrant County's fastest-growing cities (estimated 10,771 residents by mid-2024).

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW metro — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Tarrant County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data); Kennedale shares full exposure to the DFW hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Straight-line thunderstorm winds are common in spring and frequently cause shingle lift/blow-off independent of hail, affecting both Kennedale's historic core and its rapidly expanding newer subdivisions.
- **Roofing material impact**: Kennedale's rapid recent growth means a large and growing share of newer homes already carry architectural shingle from the builder; the older in-town core remains the strongest near-term market for full reroofs and Class 4 upgrades.
