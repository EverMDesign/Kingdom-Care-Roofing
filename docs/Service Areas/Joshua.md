# Joshua, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Johnson County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 7–8 miles south/southwest of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: U.S. Highway 174, FM 917; historically connected via the Fort Worth Southern Traction Line (interurban rail)
- **Population**: 9,146 (2024 estimate), up from 7,891 (2020 Census), 5,910 (2010), and 4,528 (2000) — nearly 40% growth over the past decade, among the fastest-growing cities in the Kingdom Care service area
- **Incorporation**: Became a stop on the Fort Worth Southern Traction Line (interurban service connecting Cleburne and Fort Worth) in 1912; the city had an organized municipal government by around 1949, when Theodore "Ted" Roosevelt Strube served as its first mayor
- **General character**: A fast-growing north-central Johnson County suburb directly south of Burleson, benefiting significantly from southward DFW suburban expansion along the I-35W/U.S. 174 corridor.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Joshua Independent School District — 10 schools (5 elementary, 2 middle schools, 2 high schools, 1 alternative school), serving portions of Cleburne, Burleson, Cross Timber, and Crowley in addition to Joshua itself. Joshua ISD is the third-largest district in Johnson County and a fast-growth district, with roughly 1,500 additional students projected over the next decade. Joshua High School was established in 1907 (current campus at 909 S. Broadway St.). North Joshua Elementary ranks among the top-rated elementary schools in Texas (244th of 4,651, 5-star rating).
- **Civic**: Joshua Municipal Building (City Hall), 101 S. Main St., Joshua, TX 76058.
- **Historic**: The Fort Worth Southern Traction Line connection (1912) remains a notable piece of the city's founding-era transportation history.

## 4. History

- Joshua's organized development accelerated in 1912, when the Fort Worth Southern Traction Line — an interurban rail service connecting Cleburne and Fort Worth — made Joshua a stop, spurring local settlement and commerce.
- By around 1949, the city had an organized municipal government, with Theodore "Ted" Roosevelt Strube serving as its first mayor.
- Joshua High School, established in 1907, predates the city's formal municipal organization and reflects the area's earlier rural school-community roots.
- Growth remained modest through most of the 20th century before accelerating sharply in the 2000s–2020s: from 4,528 residents in 2000 to 5,910 (2010), 7,891 (2020), and an estimated 9,146 by 2024 — nearly 40% growth in the most recent decade alone, driven by Joshua's position directly south of Burleson along the region's southward suburban growth corridor.
- Joshua ISD has grown into the third-largest school district in Johnson County, with substantial further enrollment growth projected, reflecting the broader residential expansion across the district's multi-city footprint.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW/North Texas region — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Johnson County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data); Joshua shares full exposure to the regional hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Straight-line thunderstorm winds are common in spring and frequently cause shingle lift/blow-off independent of hail, affecting both Joshua's older in-town core and its rapidly expanding newer subdivisions.
- **Roofing material impact**: Joshua's rapid growth means a large and growing share of newer homes already carry architectural shingle from the builder; the older in-town core represents the strongest near-term market for full reroofs and Class 4 upgrades.
