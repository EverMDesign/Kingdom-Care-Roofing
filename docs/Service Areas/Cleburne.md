# Cleburne, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Johnson County (county seat)
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 14–16 miles southwest of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base (straight-line ~14 miles; driving ~15.6 miles, about 25 minutes)
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: U.S. Highway 67, State Highway 171, Chisholm Trail Parkway (southern extension)
- **Population**: 31,352 (2020 Census); roughly 33,000 as of 2022 estimates; grew from under 700 (1870) to 4,000 (within 20 years of founding) and over 12,000 by 1920 — a long, steady growth trajectory as the county's commercial and rail hub
- **Incorporation**: Founded March 23, 1867; incorporated May 3, 1871; named for Confederate General Patrick Cleburne. Cleburne is Johnson County's third county seat (following Wardville, now submerged under Lake Pat Cleburne)
- **General character**: The Johnson County seat and a historic railroad town, blending a well-preserved historic downtown with a fast-growing residential base and lakeside recreation at Lake Pat Cleburne.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Historic Downtown Cleburne**: Centered around the Johnson County Courthouse; home to the oldest housing stock in the city, much of it dating to the railroad-boom era around the turn of the 20th century — strong candidates for full tear-off reroofs given age.
- **Santa Fe Shops/railroad-era residential areas**: Neighborhoods that grew up around the Santa Fe Railroad's major facility (opened 1898), generally mid-20th-century housing now on multiple generations of asphalt shingle replacement.
- **Lake Pat Cleburne-adjacent neighborhoods**: Recreational/residential areas near the lake, with a mix of housing eras.
- **Newer growth areas along the Chisholm Trail Parkway's southern extension**: Cleburne has been described as a "fast-growing city" benefiting from the Parkway's extension; new construction here is predominantly architectural shingle, with growing Class 4 impact-resistant adoption.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Cleburne Independent School District — several elementary schools, two middle schools, and Cleburne High School (home of the Yellow Jackets; Yellow Jacket Stadium is a recognized local landmark).
- **Historic/Cultural**: Layland Museum of History (housed in the historic Carnegie Library building, with roughly 50,000 artifacts and 100,000 photographs); Santa Fe Caboose and Santa Fe Shops (railroad heritage); Chisholm Trail Outdoor Museum (one-room schoolhouse, original Wardville jail doors, mule barn, and the oldest log courthouse in the state).
- **Civic/Government**: Johnson County Courthouse.
- **Religious/Community anchor**: Church of the Holy Comforter.
- **Recreation**: Lake Pat Cleburne (site of annual Independence Day Chamber celebration with vendors and food trucks).

## 4. History

- Cleburne was founded on March 23, 1867, after the western portion of Johnson County was severed to form Hood County, and the town was chosen as the new county seat — the third seat in county history, following Wardville (now beneath Lake Pat Cleburne).
- The city incorporated on May 3, 1871, and was named for Confederate General Patrick Cleburne.
- Early growth was agriculture- and county-seat-driven; the population reached about 4,000 within its first two decades.
- The arrival of the Santa Fe Railway (first tracks from Galveston reached town in 1879) transformed Cleburne's economy, and the opening of a major Santa Fe Railroad facility in 1898 cemented the city's role as a railroad hub — helping population surpass 12,000 by 1920.
- In 1900, Cleburne hosted the founding convention of the Texas State Federation of Labor, reflecting its industrial/rail significance at the time.
- More recent growth has been driven by the extension of the Chisholm Trail Parkway, connecting Cleburne more directly into the Fort Worth commuter corridor and spurring new residential development alongside continued preservation of the historic downtown.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate — hot summers, mild winters, with the DFW/North Texas region's characteristic spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Johnson County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data). Cleburne shares full exposure to the regional hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Lake Pat Cleburne-adjacent areas can see elevated straight-line wind exposure off open water during spring storms.
- **Roofing material impact**: Cleburne's mix of century-old downtown housing and rapidly expanding newer subdivisions makes it a strong market for both legacy full-reroof work and new-construction Class 4 impact-resistant shingle specification.

## 6. Roofing & Construction Industry Relevance

- **Materials by era**: Historic downtown and Santa Fe-era residential housing is largely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle, with some original wood-frame structures. Mid-to-late 20th-century neighborhoods are a mix of 3-tab and architectural shingle. Newer Chisholm Trail Parkway-adjacent growth areas are predominantly builder-grade architectural shingle with rising Class 4 adoption.
- **Replacement/upgrade sweet spot**: The city's century-plus housing history gives it one of the deepest "due for reroof" markets in the Kingdom Care service area, concentrated in and around historic downtown and the older railroad-era neighborhoods.
- **Insurance claim patterns**: Johnson County's top-ten ranking for wind/hail claims applies directly; Cleburne, as the county seat and a dense population center, likely sees proportionally high claim volume within the county.
- **HOA considerations**: Newer Chisholm Trail Parkway-corridor subdivisions may carry HOA architectural review requirements; older downtown and railroad-era neighborhoods are generally non-HOA.
- **Permit authority**: City of Cleburne Building Inspection Department / Community Development — phone **(817) 645-0955**.
- **Code requirements**: Texas has no mandatory statewide residential building code; the state adopted the IRC as a default standard, but each incorporated city enforces its own adopted edition. Cleburne, as an incorporated city and county seat, administers its own building permit and inspection requirements — confirm current code edition with Community Development before scoping work.
