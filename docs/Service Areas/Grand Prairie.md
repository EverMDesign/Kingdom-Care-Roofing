# Grand Prairie, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Primarily Dallas County, with portions extending into Tarrant County and Ellis County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 33 miles northeast of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base (driving distance)
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Interstate 30, Interstate 20, State Highway 161, State Highway 360
- **Population**: 2024 estimate 201,883 (17th most populous city in Texas), up 2.69% year-over-year; grew from 994 (1910) to 1,263 (1920), 1,529 (1930), 1,595 (1940), and has grown 57.8% since the 2000 census — a major population center within the Kingdom Care service radius
- **Incorporation**: First established as "Dechman" by Alexander McRae Dechman in 1863 as a stagecoach stop between Dallas and Fort Worth; renamed Grand Prairie by the railroad in 1877 for its location on the eastern edge of the vast grand prairie stretching into West Texas; officially incorporated as the town of Grand Prairie on April 15, 1909, with approximately 1,000 residents
- **General character**: A major DFW entertainment, recreation, and residential hub — one of the largest cities in the Kingdom Care service area, combining historic roots with significant post-WWII and ongoing modern growth.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Historic core near Uptown Theater and the original Dechman/Grand Prairie townsite**: Oldest housing stock in the city, likely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle.
- **Post-WWII growth neighborhoods**: Built out as returning veterans sought housing and employment, reflecting the city's major mid-20th-century expansion; largely 3-tab and early architectural shingle.
- **Lakeview Pointe and other newer master-planned communities (including areas toward Fort Worth)**: Recent new-construction development, predominantly architectural shingle with growing Class 4 impact-resistant adoption.
- **Neighborhoods near Lone Star Park and EpicCentral**: Mixed residential development surrounding the city's major entertainment/recreation investments.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Grand Prairie is served by several school districts given its size and multi-county footprint; the city has invested significantly in schools, libraries, and educational programs as part of its family-oriented identity.

## 4. History

- Grand Prairie's origins trace to 1863, when Alexander McRae Dechman established the community (then named "Dechman") as a stagecoach stop between Dallas and Fort Worth.
- In 1877, the railroad renamed the town Grand Prairie, reflecting its position on the eastern edge of the vast grand prairie extending into West Texas.
- The town officially incorporated on April 15, 1909, with a population of roughly 1,000.
- Early growth was slow and steady (994 in 1910 to 1,595 by 1940), with the city functioning primarily as a farming community through the early 20th century.
- Rapid growth followed World War II, as returning veterans sought housing and employment opportunities, setting the city on a path toward its current scale.
- The 1997 opening of Lone Star Park marked a deliberate shift toward recreation and entertainment as an economic driver, followed by continued investment culminating in EpicCentral's modern recreation and civic facilities.
- Since 2000, Grand Prairie has grown 57.8%, reaching nearly 202,000 residents by 2024 and establishing itself as the 17th most populous city in Texas.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW metro — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Dallas and Tarrant Counties are both consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data); Grand Prairie's position spanning both counties means full exposure to the DFW hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: As one of the larger, denser cities in the service area, Grand Prairie's mix of mature and newer neighborhoods sees the full range of straight-line wind-related shingle damage typical of the DFW metro.
- **Roofing material impact**: Given the city's enormous size and mixed-age housing stock, Grand Prairie represents one of the deepest and most diverse roofing markets in the Kingdom Care service area — from legacy full-reroof opportunities in the historic core to new-construction Class 4 impact-resistant specification in newer developments.

## 6. Roofing & Construction Industry Relevance

- **Materials by era**: Historic core and post-WWII neighborhoods are largely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle; later 20th-century growth areas are a mix of 3-tab and architectural shingle; newer master-planned communities (e.g., Lakeview Pointe) are predominantly builder-grade architectural shingle with rising Class 4 adoption.
- **Replacement/upgrade sweet spot**: Given the city's long, continuous growth history, Grand Prairie offers both a deep legacy-reroof market in its older sections and an active new-construction material-specification market in its newer developments.
- **Insurance claim patterns**: Both Dallas and Tarrant Counties' top-ten rankings for wind/hail claims apply to different parts of the city; Grand Prairie's large population and housing stock make it a high-volume restoration market overall.
- **HOA considerations**: Newer master-planned subdivisions commonly carry HOA architectural review requirements — confirm CC&Rs on shingle type/color before quoting upgrades; older historic-core neighborhoods are generally non-HOA.
- **Permit authority**: City of Grand Prairie Building Inspections Division, 300 W. Main St., Grand Prairie, TX 75050 — phone **(972) 237-8230**. Office hours Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–5 p.m.
- **Code requirements**: Texas has no mandatory statewide residential building code; the state adopted the IRC as a default standard, but each incorporated city enforces its own adopted edition. Grand Prairie, as a large incorporated city, administers its own building permit and inspection requirements — confirm current code edition with the Building Inspections Division before scoping work.
