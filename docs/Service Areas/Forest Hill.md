# Forest Hill, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Tarrant County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 12.7 miles north of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base (roughly equidistant between Burleson and Arlington)
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Interstate 20, East California Parkway, near the southeast edge of Fort Worth
- **Population**: 2024 estimate 14,314; grew from 10,250 (1976) to 11,482 (1990), 11,326 (2000), 12,353 (2010), and 13,956 (2020) — steady, sustained growth over nearly five decades
- **Incorporation**: Community origins trace to around 1860, known successively as Brambleton Station and Forest Hill Village before taking the Forest Hill name; the community had its first schools by 1896 and officially incorporated in 1946. The city adopted a Home Rule Charter in the early 1970s to support annexation and governance.
- **General character**: A mature, long-established suburb on Fort Worth's southeast side, split between two school districts, with a mix of mid-century residential neighborhoods and ongoing modest growth.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Neighborhoods served by Fort Worth ISD (Harlean Beal Elementary and David K. Sellars Elementary areas)**: Established residential areas likely dating to the city's mid-20th-century growth period, with multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle common.
- **Neighborhoods served by Everman ISD**: The city is split between Everman ISD and Fort Worth ISD coverage areas, each with its own pocket of established housing stock.
- **Post-1970s Home Rule Charter-era growth areas**: Development following the city's 1970s annexation push, generally a mix of 3-tab and early architectural shingle given the era.

## 4. History

- The community that became Forest Hill originated around 1860, first known as Brambleton Station and later Forest Hill Village, before settling on the Forest Hill name.
- By 1896, the community had established its first schools and was functioning as a recognized suburb of Fort Worth.
- Forest Hill officially incorporated in 1946, formalizing decades of settlement.
- By the early 1970s, the city adopted a Home Rule Charter specifically to enable easier annexation of surrounding territory and improve municipal governance, reflecting the broader wave of small Tarrant County suburbs consolidating their footprint during this period.
- Population growth has been slow but steady ever since — from just over 10,000 in the mid-1970s to over 14,000 by the mid-2020s.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW metro — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Tarrant County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data); Forest Hill shares full exposure to the DFW hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Forest Hill's mature mid-century neighborhoods typically carry established tree canopy, adding debris-related roof damage risk during high-wind events.
- **Roofing material impact**: Given the city's largely mature, built-out housing stock, the market here skews toward full tear-off reroof and Class 4 impact-resistant upgrade conversations.

## 6. Roofing & Construction Industry Relevance

- **Materials by era**: Forest Hill's mid-20th-century core housing (both Fort Worth ISD and Everman ISD coverage areas) is largely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle; 1970s–1990s Home Rule-era growth areas are a mix of 3-tab and early architectural shingle.
- **Replacement/upgrade sweet spot**: With most housing now 40–60+ years old, Forest Hill represents a deep, steady legacy-reroof market with strong potential for full tear-off and Class 4 impact-resistant upgrades.
