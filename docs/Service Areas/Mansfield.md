# Mansfield, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Primarily Tarrant County, with small portions extending into Johnson and Ellis Counties
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 13 miles east of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: U.S. Highway 287, State Highway 360; the city covers 36.66 square miles
- **Population**: 2024 American Community Survey 5-year estimate: 77,510; 2020 Census: 72,602; city budget projections estimate approaching 86,774 by end of FY 2023-24 and 91,546 by end of FY 2024-25 — one of the fastest-growing cities in the Kingdom Care service area
- **Incorporation**: Founded around 1856 by R.S. Man and Julian Feild, who built a grist mill that became the center of town (the city's name blends the founders' names); incorporated in 1890
- **General character**: A rapidly growing suburban city anchored by master-planned neighborhoods, highly rated schools, and an extensive parks system, transformed over more than 125 years from a rural farming community into a bustling suburb of more than 77,000 residents.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Historic Downtown Mansfield (near North Walnut Creek Drive and East Broad Street)**: Home to the city's oldest landmarks, including the 1924 Mansfield ISD Administration building and the 1940 WPA-built "Rock Gym" (the city's first gymnasium) — both still standing; housing in this core area likely carries multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle.
- **Master-planned neighborhoods citywide**: Mansfield's contemporary reputation is built on master-planned residential development, anchored by amenities like the Walnut Creek Linear Trail, Elmer W. Oliver Nature Park, and nearby recreation (Hawaiian Falls, Big League Dreams, Mansfield National Golf Course, Fieldhouse USA, StarCenter); newer construction here is predominantly architectural (dimensional) shingle, with Class 4 impact-resistant options increasingly specified.
- **Rapid-growth corridor neighborhoods**: Reflecting the city's accelerating population growth (72,602 in 2020 toward a projected 91,546+ by 2025), new subdivisions continue to be developed across the city.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Mansfield Independent School District — 49 schools serving more than 35,000 students (one pre-K academy, 24 elementary schools, seven intermediate schools, seven middle schools, two standalone STEM academies, and multiple high schools); one of the fastest-growing districts in Texas, with near-constant new school construction. In 1956, a federal court ordered Mansfield ISD to desegregate — the first such order in Texas.
- **Historic/Cultural**: The Mansfield ISD Administration building (built 1924), the WPA-built "Rock Gym" (1940), and the Man House Museum; the Mansfield Historical Society is working to erect a statue and gazebo at North Walnut Creek Drive and East Broad Street.
- **Parks/Recreation**: Walnut Creek Linear Trail, Elmer W. Oliver Nature Park, Hawaiian Falls, Big League Dreams, Mansfield National Golf Course, Fieldhouse USA, StarCenter.
- **Civic**: Mansfield Chamber of Commerce, located on Main Street in Downtown Mansfield.

## 4. History

- Mansfield was founded around 1856 by R.S. Man and Julian Feild, who built a grist mill after Feild purchased 540 acres of land — the mill became the center of the early town, and the city's name is a blend of the two founders' names.
- The town incorporated in 1890, continuing as a hub for the surrounding farmland for decades.
- In 1956, Mansfield ISD was the subject of the first federal court order to desegregate a school district in Texas — a significant milestone in the city's civil rights history.
- Over more than 125 years, Mansfield transformed from a rural farming community into a major suburban city, with growth accelerating sharply in recent decades — from 72,602 residents in 2020 to a projected 91,546+ by the 2024-25 fiscal year.
- The city has invested heavily in master-planned neighborhood development, parks, and one of the fastest-growing school districts in the state, while preserving historic landmarks like the 1924 ISD Administration building and the 1940 WPA Rock Gym.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW metro — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Tarrant County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data); Mansfield shares full exposure to the DFW hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Straight-line thunderstorm winds are common in spring and frequently cause shingle lift/blow-off independent of hail, affecting both Mansfield's historic core and its rapidly expanding newer subdivisions.
- **Roofing material impact**: Mansfield's rapid growth means a large and growing share of newer homes already carry architectural shingle from the builder; the historic downtown core represents the strongest near-term market for full reroofs and Class 4 upgrades.

## 6. Roofing & Construction Industry Relevance

- **Materials by era**: Historic downtown Mansfield housing is largely on multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle; master-planned neighborhoods built over the past two to three decades are predominantly architectural shingle; the most recent rapid-growth subdivisions increasingly specify Class 4 impact-resistant shingle.
- **Replacement/upgrade sweet spot**: With such explosive recent population growth, Mansfield offers both a legacy-reroof market in its historic core and a very active new-construction material-specification market citywide.
- **Insurance claim patterns**: Tarrant County's top-ten ranking for wind/hail claims applies directly; Mansfield's rapidly expanding rooftop count means claim volume is likely rising in step with its growing housing stock.
