# Duncanville, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Dallas County
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 25 miles northeast of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: Interstate 20, U.S. Highway 67, FM 1382 (Wheatland Road)
- **Population**: 40,706 (2020 Census); grew from 113 (1904) to over 300 (1933), 5,000 (1962), 13,000 (1970), and over 31,000 (1988) — rapid mid-20th-century growth driven by proximity to expanding Dallas
- **Incorporation**: Settlement began in 1845 when Illinois resident Crawford Trees purchased several thousand acres south of Camp Dallas; the city incorporated on August 2, 1947 (initially 225 acres), specifically to prevent annexation by the expanding City of Dallas, with D.L. Hopkins Jr. as first mayor
- **General character**: A well-established southwest Dallas County suburb and founding member of the "Best Southwest Cities" partnership, known statewide as a basketball powerhouse community alongside its mature, settled residential base.

## 2. Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

- **Established residential areas throughout the city**: Duncanville's rapid 1950s–1960s growth (5,000 in 1962 to 13,000 by 1970) means much of its core housing stock is now 55–70+ years old — a deep legacy-reroof market with likely multiple generations of 3-tab asphalt shingle replacement already completed.
- **1970s–1980s growth-era neighborhoods**: Built out as the city passed 31,000 residents by 1988; a mix of 3-tab and early architectural shingle.
- **Newer infill and redevelopment areas**: Limited new-construction land remains given the city's largely built-out, mature footprint; where present, newer homes trend toward architectural shingle.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Duncanville Independent School District — more than 11,500 students across 18 campuses (one early learning academy, nine elementary schools, four middle schools, one high school, and two alternative campuses); 13 of 17 TEA-rated schools are rated Exemplary or Recognized.
- **Historic/Cultural**: Duncanville Historical Park, located on Wheatland Road in Armstrong Park on land that was once part of the former Duncanville Air Force Station; most city services are consolidated at this facility, which also houses the Duncanville Recreation Center (meeting rooms, double gymnasium, indoor walking track).
- **Civic/Memorial**: Memorial Park at Main Street and Center Street, dedicated in 1969 as a war memorial.
- **Community identity**: Duncanville is widely known as a basketball powerhouse — often cited as the "basketball capital of Texas," hosting multiple tournaments throughout the year.

## 4. History

- Settlement of the Duncanville area began in 1845, when Illinois resident Crawford Trees purchased several thousand acres south of Camp Dallas.
- The city incorporated on August 2, 1947, initially encompassing just 225 acres — a deliberate move by residents to prevent annexation by the rapidly expanding City of Dallas. D.L. Hopkins Jr. served as the city's first mayor.
- Growth accelerated dramatically in the postwar decades as Dallas-area suburban development expanded outward: from 5,000 residents in 1962 to 13,000 by 1970, and over 31,000 by 1988.
- The population has since stabilized, reaching 40,706 at the 2020 Census, reflecting a largely built-out, mature suburban footprint.
- Duncanville became one of four founding partner communities in the "Best Southwest Cities" regional collaboration, and its school district and recreational facilities (including the historic Air Force Station site now home to Duncanville Historical Park) continue to anchor community identity.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW metro — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Dallas County is a historically high-frequency hail county within the DFW region, sharing full exposure to major regional events including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Duncanville's mature, long-settled tree canopy (typical of a city largely built out by the 1980s) adds debris-related roof damage risk during high-wind events.
- **Roofing material impact**: Given the city's older, mature housing stock, the market here skews heavily toward full tear-off reroof and Class 4 impact-resistant upgrade conversations rather than new-construction material specification.
