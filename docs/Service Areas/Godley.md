# Godley, TX — Service Area Research Profile

## 1. City Overview

- **County**: Johnson County (small portions of the surrounding school district extend into Hood and Tarrant Counties)
- **Location relative to Kingdom Care Roofing & Construction**: Approximately 13 miles south/southwest of Kingdom Care's Burleson, TX base, along the Highway 171 corridor
- **Nearest major roads/highways**: State Highway 171
- **Population**: Explosive recent growth — approximately 1,441 (2020 Census) to an estimated 5,093 by 2025 (+139.5% growth over the decade); historically grew slowly from 747 (1990) to 1,092 (2000) and 1,306 (2010)
- **Incorporation**: Began in 1886 when rancher and lumber merchant B.B. Godley donated land for a townsite and railroad right-of-way to the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railroad; incorporated as a municipality in 1913, transitioning from the unincorporated railroad community to a Type A general-law city with a council-mayor government
- **General character**: A small but rapidly growing Johnson County town directly in the path of southward residential expansion from Burleson and Crowley — one of the fastest-growing cities in the entire Kingdom Care service radius.

## 3. Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

- **Schools**: Godley Independent School District — a rural district covering more than 122 square miles, mostly in Johnson County with small areas extending into Hood and Tarrant Counties. The historic Godley school complex (Godley College, begun in 1899, became Godley High School by 1902; a three-story brick building opened in time for 1916 graduation) was designated a Recorded Texas Historic Landmark in 2010 and still houses Godley Intermediate School and the Godley ISD Administration Offices.
- **Historic/Cultural**: The historic Godley school building itself functions as a recognized community landmark.
- **Civic anchor**: Godley Chamber of Commerce, 8841 N. Highway 171, Godley, TX 76044.

## 4. History

- The town of Godley began in 1886, when rancher and lumber merchant B.B. Godley donated land for a townsite and railroad right-of-way to the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railroad.
- Johnson County Commissioners Court formed the Godley Independent School District in July 1884, even before the town's formal founding, reflecting early organized settlement in the area (driven by agricultural pioneers farming land near Deer Creek in the 1880s).
- Godley incorporated as a municipality in 1913, becoming a Type A general-law city with a council-mayor government.
- Godley College opened in 1899 and became Godley High School by 1902; a three-story brick school building completed in time for the 1916 graduation later earned Recorded Texas Historic Landmark status in 2010.
- The town remained small and slow-growing for over a century (747 residents in 1990 to 1,441 by 2020), but has experienced a dramatic recent growth surge — reaching an estimated 5,093 residents by 2025 — as southward residential expansion from Burleson and Crowley along the Highway 171 corridor reached the town.

## 5. Weather & Storm Patterns

- **Climate baseline**: Humid subtropical climate consistent with the greater DFW/North Texas region — hot summers, mild winters, active spring/early-summer severe weather season.
- **Hail risk**: Johnson County is consistently ranked among the top Texas counties for wind and hail insurance claims (2007–2017 data); Godley shares full exposure to the regional hail corridor, including the record 2016 North Texas hail season (~500,000 statewide claims, $4+ billion in insured losses).
- **Tornado risk**: The DFW metro has recorded roughly 350 tornadoes since 1952; the December 26, 2015 Garland–Rowlett EF4 outbreak ($1.55 billion in damage) remains the costliest tornado event in metro history.
- **Wind**: Godley's rural, open surrounding terrain offers less windbreak than denser suburban areas, meaning straight-line thunderstorm winds can have a more direct impact on both older in-town homes and newer rooftop construction.
- **Roofing material impact**: Godley's explosive new-construction growth makes Class 4 impact-resistant shingle specification highly relevant at the point of new-home build, while the town's older historic core remains a smaller but real legacy-reroof opportunity.
